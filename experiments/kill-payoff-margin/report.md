# kill-payoff-margin: comparison report

**Question:** Does a richer kill give meat eaters the margin they lack in the grand world? A body holds bodyEnergyPerSize units of energy per unit of size (4 today; making a baby costs 30), carved out of what the parent hands its baby, so richer bodies mean richer carcasses but newborns with less spare energy. Today's 4 against 6, 8 and 12, with one animal in twenty becoming an armed meat eater at tick 100,000 and every 5,000 ticks after, measured with the diet ledger. An answer: meat eaters' births against deaths, income and costs, and how many remain 4,500 ticks after each arrival, against the plant eaters' births.

Base world `grand-world`; 150,000 ticks; seeds 1901-1904; 3 arms against `control`; 16 of 16 runs finished.

How to read this: each arm is compared with the control on the same seeds. *Change* is the average difference, *95% range* is where the true change probably lies, and *noise units* is the change divided by the control's spread between seeds (1 = as big as the usual difference between two seeds). Measures are taken over the second half of each run unless named otherwise. With many arms, a few will look important by chance alone, so every finding here is re-run on new seeds before anything is changed.
